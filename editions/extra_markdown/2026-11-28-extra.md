# The Story: Hopper Deadlock + Loophole Tightening Results in NeoCloud Squeeze

## What Happened

### 1. The U.S.–China Hopper Deadlock (April 2025 – Mid-2026)

* **The U.S. Licensing Pivot (April 2025 – January 2026):** While cutting-edge Blackwell architectures remained strictly banned under a presumption of denial, Washington shifted toward transactional licensing for Hopper silicon. Following initial curbs in April 2025, the U.S. Department of Commerce created case-by-case licensing frameworks for H20 chips (July 2025) and top-tier H200s (January 2026) under specific volume caps and fee structures.
* **Beijing’s Counter-Move (July 2025 – Mid-2026):** Despite U.S. export approvals, Chinese regulatory bodies—including the Cyberspace Administration of China (CAC) and MIIT—issued informal "window guidance" directing domestic tech giants to halt foreign chip procurement and transition workloads to local alternatives like Huawei’s Ascend series.
* **Logistical Freeze & Loophole Closures (May 2026 – Late 2026):** Hundreds of thousands of licensed Hopper units stalled in transit hubs. Simultaneously, the U.S. Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) closed foreign subsidiary loopholes and clamped down on offshore data centers renting remote GPU capacity to Chinese end-users. Nvidia subsequently halted licensed shipments to mainland buyers and excluded China data-center sales from forward guidance.

### 2. Collateral Risk in NeoCloud Debt Structures

Specialized AI cloud providers ("NeoClouds") financed rapid cluster expansion using private credit and asset-backed debt structured through bankruptcy-remote Special Purpose Vehicles (SPVs):

* **The Maturity & Depreciation Mismatch:** Facilities carry 4- to 5-year loan amortizations against hardware facing fast generational obsolescence from incoming Blackwell clusters. Furthermore, secondary salvage value projections had relied on an open Chinese secondary market as the primary volume buyer for decommissioned Hopper hardware.
* **Borrowing Base Mechanics:**
* SPV facilities calculate borrowing capacity using Orderly Liquidation Value (OLV) and enforce rapid depreciation schedules (often amortizing hardware collateral value down to zero within 36 to 48 months).
* Collateral values are marked down via mandatory quarterly third-party technical appraisals.

* **Covenants and Cash Waterfalls:**
* **Loan-to-Value (LTV) Caps:** Ceilings set around 65%–75% of OLV require borrowers to inject cash, add hardware collateral, or prepay principal if secondary market appraisals drop.
* **DSCR & Cash Sweeps:** Collateral write-downs combined with declining spot-market rental rates depress Debt Service Coverage Ratios (DSCR below ~1.25x). This triggers 100% cash sweeps, trapping all net operating cash flow inside the SPV to amortize senior debt and cutting off equity distributions to the parent NeoCloud.

---

### 3. Financial Fallout: Covenant Adjustments Over Foreclosures

The evaporation of the Chinese secondary market, combined with curbs on offshore remote leasing to Chinese entities, drove collateral revaluations across spot-exposed SPVs:

* **Structural Triggers Fired:** Spot-dependent facilities tripped LTV covenants and triggered cash sweeps as hourly Hopper rental yields softened.
* **Why Liquidations Were Avoided:** Physical silicon is illiquid and depreciates continuously without energized data center space and power purchase agreements (PPAs). Rather than seizing physical clusters, private debt syndicates favored covenant waivers, higher credit spreads, and asset roll-ups into well-capitalized hyperscalers or infrastructure funds.

## Why the Narrative Stayed Quiet

* **The Private Credit Black Box:** GPU-backed debt is concentrated in private credit funds and alternative asset managers rather than on public bank balance sheets. These loans do not trade on public exchanges, avoiding daily mark-to-market disclosures and allowing lenders to manage covenant breaches privately through "amend and extend" restructurings.
* **Hyperscaler Ring-Fencing:** The largest NeoCloud debt facilities are shielded by multi-year, take-or-pay off-take agreements from investment-grade hyperscalers (e.g., Microsoft, Meta). Because the underlying credit risk is the corporate counterparty rather than the liquidation value of the chips, these high-grade facilities showed no public strain.
* **Shared Institutional Incentives:** Hardware vendors, cloud operators preparing for public offerings, and private credit managers all share commercial incentives to downplay secondary hardware depreciation and maintain high fleet valuations.
* **Media Silos:** The story unfolded across three non-intersecting beats—national security reporters covered trade controls, tech outlets tracked model training and frontier chips, and debt newsletters monitored private SPV waterfalls—preventing the issue from coalescing into a single mainstream financial headline.
