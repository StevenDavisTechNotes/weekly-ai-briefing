# Statement of Intent

I built this weekly brief for myself and decided to share it. Most AI coverage repeats the consensus. This brief looks only for what changed: the developments that last week's consensus did not predict, and what they imply for the next four years.

Each issue is researched and drafted by an AI model working from a fixed prompt that I maintain, and every issue lists its sources and any discrepancies between them. My vantage point is enterprise software architecture and institutional finance, especially fixed income, so the brief follows AI from research labs and society all the way to credit markets, including the capital build-outs the revolution requires. I read it as a technologist, an investor, and a citizen taking part in the revolution.

## How each issue is built

- **Entropy, week over week:** findings are ranked by how surprising they are, not by how big the headline is. Vendor partnerships, funding rounds, and press releases are left out on purpose.
- **So what:** each finding ends with a first-derivative extrapolation, meaning where this goes if the trend continues.
- **Forecast revision:** the brief says how each finding changes the existing outlook, and "Strategic Mental Model Shifts" collects those revisions in one place.

## What each issue covers

- **The Five Signals That Matter This Week:** the five least predictable developments, drawn from any section.
- **AI Technological Paradigm Shifts:** surprises in optimization, training dynamics, inference scaling, reasoning beyond human-readable chain of thought, evaluation, and alignment.
- **Quantum Computing:** verified operational breakthroughs, error correction, quantum-classical hybrids, AI for quantum and quantum for AI, and readiness for post-quantum cryptography.
- **Enterprise AI Adoption:** cloud versus edge inference economics, architecture for agentic services, declarative UX, agent identity, governance, and deployer liability.
- **Physical AI & Industrial Automation:** learned robot policies and world models entering factory and warehouse OT, how deterministic control bounds them, the cost and reliability thresholds for adoption, the shape of the capital build-out, and OT resilience only as it matters to investors and systemic risk. The perspective is that of an investor, not an OT builder or security researcher.
- **Societal Adoption:** psychological, educational, sociological, employment, environmental, and political developments affecting or affected by the AI revolution, from companion toys and changing universities to retooling workers and movements for and against AI.
- **Economic Structural Change:** productivity, inflation and interest rates, labor-market aggregates, energy and power supply, supply chains, the capital-spending cycle, trade and industrial policy, and macro-financial stability, with the evidence quality stated.
- **Institutional Financial Services:** AI as an issuer and credit exposure, financing structures for the build-outs, newly emerging investment themes, new risk channels such as agentic deposit flows, effects on operating models and fees, and the credit and portfolio consequences of the sections above.
- **Strategic Mental Model Shifts:** how the overall picture has changed since the last issue.
- **Source Notes and Attribution:** sources, dates, limits, and attribution of each week's research.

*This brief is for information only and is not investment advice.*
