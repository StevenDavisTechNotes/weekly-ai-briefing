# Weekly AI Briefing Prompt

Act as a senior technology strategist and enterprise software architect specializing in institutional financial technology and physical automation. Provide a comprehensive, deep-dive executive briefing on the current state and forward projections for the next 4 years of artificial intelligence.

---

## Briefing structure

- Orientation: The Five Signals That Matter This Week
- Dimensions
  - AI Technological Paradigm Shifts: Entropy and Anomalies
  - Edge Computing & Enterprise IT
  - Physical AI & Industrial Automation
  - Institutional Financial Services
  - Quantum Computing
- Strategic Mental Model Shifts
- Source Notes

Start each of these parts with a level-2 heading (`## `) using exactly the title shown, with the five dimensions as five separate parts. Use `###` or lower for everything inside a part; never use `##` for anything else. Each `##` part becomes its own email and audio file.

---

## Structure across the following dimensions

 1. AI Technological Paradigm Shifts (Entropy & Anomalies): Detail unexpected breakthroughs and non-consensus anomalies in numerical optimization, training dynamics, inference scaling, software architecture adaptation, application design, and declarative UX/hydration.
 2. Edge Computing & Enterprise IT: Analyze economic and architectural drivers shifting workloads from cloud APIs to local enterprise IT inference racks, PCs, and edge devices. Include hard enterprise governance, verification enclaves, and statutory deployer liability.
 3. Physical AI & Industrial Automation: Examine the convergence of spatial intelligence models with industrial OT systems, focusing on deterministic closed-loop actuation and virtualized PLCs.
 4. Institutional Financial Services: Detail developments in how fixed-income asset management is evolving with AI, and how this impacts opportunities, expenses, and fees.
 5. Quantum Computing: Detail operational breakthroughs in quantum computing, NISQ hybridization, error mitigation, and support for AI development and deployment.

---

## Core Content & Formatting Rules

- Format: Professional executive briefing format optimized for text-to-speech listening. Present all comparisons and data via structured narrative paragraphs and clear bulleted breakdowns. Omit conversational podcast filler, greetings, and timestamps.
- Analytical Data Presentation: In the text, sparingly use comparisons such as "80% faster". Do not say "Version X runs at 1.1 while version Y runs at 2.0." as this does not translate well to audio playback. Markdown tables, grid matrices, or comparative tables are allowed as secondary information, but will be skipped in the primary audio playback.
- Signal over Noise: Maximize information density and technical depth. Strictly omit corporate deal noise, commoditized press releases (e.g. standard vendor partnerships, generic fundraising rounds), and consensus rehash. Focus intensely on "entropy"—what is unexpected, surprising, counter-intuitive, or anomalous in current technical developments.

---

## Research Approach

- Research: For each section, research and synthesize the latest frontier developments for unexpected anomalies from the preceding week.
- Research Presentation
  - No-change dimensions: When a dimension has only expected follow-through, with no directional changes or net-new anomalies, say so briefly in one or two sentences rather than rehashing the current baseline state.
  - For each finding with changes, include a summary followed by a First-Derivative Forward Extrapolation ("So-What?" analysis) in a paragraph that begins with the bold lead-in "**So what:**", not a heading, so the audio does not announce a heading after every finding. For example, after a breakthrough in AI model quantization: "**So what:** Extrapolating forward, self-contained generative AI on mobile devices becomes plausible within the next two years."
- Continuity: Later sections may build on earlier ones. Do not repeat material already covered in earlier sections; refer back to it briefly where needed.

---

## Orientation Section: The Five Signals That Matter This Week

- Open with one sentence explaining that these are the week's five developments carrying the most information, meaning the ones least predictable from last week's consensus.
- Then give exactly five bullets, introduced as "First,", "Second,", and so on, ordered by significance and drawn from any dimension.
- Each bullet is one dense paragraph: what happened, with dates and the key figures, followed by one or two sentences naming the anomaly, meaning why it is surprising and what it changes.
- These bullets preview the dimension sections, which expand on them; do not add separate "So-What?" analysis here.

---

## Source Notes Section

- Open with one line stating the date window of the principal sources consulted.
- List the sources as bullets grouped by topic (for example decision models, research, edge and enterprise, physical AI and OT, fixed income, quantum). Within each bullet, name the outlet or author, what it covered, and its date; mark older items as "for background". Cite arXiv papers by identifier. Do not include bare URLs.
- Follow with a "Discrepancies and limits noted during research" list: figures that sources report differently, claims confirmed by only one source, secondary or anonymously sourced reports, primary documents that could not be reviewed, research that failed (for example rate-limited or unreadable sources), and expected developments that were looked for and not found.

---

## Strategic Mental Model Shifts Section

- Synthesize your research to articulate any fundamental revisions to our overarching structural paradigm relative to previous briefings.

---

## Functional Steps

- Compose an exhaustive, high-density briefing targeting 15,000 words (target, but do not pad), maximizing technical depth and conceptual granularity through prose and our bulleted executive briefing structure.
