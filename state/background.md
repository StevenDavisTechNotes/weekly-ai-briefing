# Background

## B-01 The Hopper licensing deadlock with China

- Underlies: M-09; Nvidia position as central supplier.
- Milestones:
  - 2025-04-09: The US government told Nvidia that H20 exports to China and D:5 countries need a license, and on April 14 that the requirement would last indefinitely. Nvidia expected up to about $5.5 billion of charges and recorded $4.5 billion. Source: Nvidia, Form 8-K, 2025-04-15, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581025000082/nvda-20250409.htm; Nvidia, Form 10-Q, 2025-08-27, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581025000209/nvda-20250727.htm
  - 2025-07-31: The Cyberspace Administration of China summoned Nvidia and told it to explain the "backdoor" security risks of H20 chips sold to China. Washington had allowed H20 sales to resume about two weeks earlier. Source: CAC statement, 2025-07-31, https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-07/31/c_1755675743897163.htm; The Register, "Beijing summons Nvidia over alleged backdoors in China-bound AI chips", 2025-07-31, https://www.theregister.com/2025/07/31/beijing_nvidia_backdoors/
  - 2025-08 to 2025-10: The US granted H20 licenses, and officials said they expected 15 percent "or more" of licensed revenue, a share no regulation has codified. Nvidia made about $50 million of H20 revenue under those licenses by the quarter ended 2025-10-26. Source: Nvidia, Form 10-Q, 2025-11-19, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581025000230/nvda-20251026.htm
  - 2026-01-15: A BIS final rule moved H200-class exports to China from a presumption of denial to case-by-case review. China's shipments are capped at 50 percent of the aggregate TPP shipped to US customers. Each shipment needs independent testing in the US, which brings a 25 percent import tariff, and screening against remote access. Source: BIS, "Revision to License Review Policy for Advanced Computing Commodities", Federal Register 2026-00789, 2026-01-15, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-01-15/html/2026-00789.htm; for the tariff, Nvidia, Form 10-Q, 2026-05-20, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000052/nvda-20260426.htm
  - 2026-08-26: Nvidia said that H200 sales under its US licenses "were restricted by the PRC government". It took a $0.4 billion H200 charge in the first half of its fiscal 2027, and licensed shipments were under 1 percent of Data Center revenue. Source: Nvidia, Form 10-Q, 2026-08-26, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm
- Now: Washington licenses Hopper-class sales and Beijing blocks them, so the deadlock now runs through Chinese policy. Nvidia's fourth-quarter guidance of 2026-11-17 assumes no China data-center compute revenue.
- Ends when: Nvidia reports, in a 10-Q or quarterly results release, licensed data-center sales to China above 5 percent of Data Center revenue in a quarter; or BIS returns H200-class exports to China to a presumption of denial in the Federal Register.

## B-02 Offshore compute and the remote-access gap

- Underlies: M-09 (tenant veto); AI credit and data-center debt window; Nvidia position as central supplier.
- Milestones:
  - 2025-05-13: BIS announced it would rescind the 2025-01-15 AI Diffusion Rule and stopped enforcing it. It also imposed licenses for AI chips to Malaysia and Thailand, citing known diversion, and warned against letting US chips train Chinese models. Source: BIS press release, "Department of Commerce Announces Rescission of Biden-Era Artificial Intelligence Diffusion Rule", 2025-05-13, https://www.bis.gov/press-release/department-commerce-announces-rescission-biden-era-artificial-intelligence-diffusion-rule-strengthens
  - 2025-07-14: Malaysia made all exports, transshipments and transits of US-origin high-performance AI chips subject to a Strategic Trade Permit with immediate effect. Under the catch-all provision, traders must notify at least 30 days ahead. Source: Malaysia MITI, press statement, 2025-07-14, https://www.miti.gov.my/miti/resources/Media%20Release/[FINAL]%5FMITI%5FPress%5FStmt%5FMalaysia%5FRegulates%5FTrade%5Fof%5FUS%5FAI%5FChips%5F2025-07-14.pdf
  - 2025-11-10: BIS suspended for one year its Affiliates Rule of 2025-09-30, which extended Entity List controls to companies at least 50 percent owned by listed parties. On 2026-11-09 it extended the suspension to 2027-01-10. Source: BIS, Federal Register 2025-19846, 2025-11-12, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-11-12/html/2025-19846.htm; BIS, Federal Register 2026-22793 (public inspection), https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-22793.pdf
  - 2026-11-19: An interim final rule required licenses for advanced chips going to overseas data centers that supply more than 12,000,000 aggregate TPP to companies headquartered in D:5 countries or Macau. It also required licenses for Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil, with US-headquartered buyers exempt subject to reporting, and it formally rescinded the Diffusion Rule. Source: BIS, "Addressing Remote Access to Advanced Semiconductors", Federal Register 2026-23529 (public inspection), https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-23529.pdf
- Now: The rule closes the route of renting offshore capacity to Chinese tenants, and lenders have frozen or stalled China-linked loans in Southeast Asia (M-09). Comments on the rule are due 60 days after publication. Providers already serving such tenants need licenses within 90 days. The Affiliates Rule returns on 2027-01-10 unless the suspension is extended again.
- Ends when: the Federal Register records both of these: BIS reinstates or rescinds the Affiliates Rule, and it issues a final rule replacing interim rule 2026-23529.

## B-03 GPU-backed neocloud debt

- Underlies: M-08; W-02; AI credit and data-center debt window.
- Milestones:
  - 2023-07: A CoreWeave subsidiary entered DDTL 1.0, the first of CoreWeave's delayed-draw term loans secured on GPU assets and contracted cash flows, for up to $2.3 billion. Source: CoreWeave, Form 10-Q, 2026-11-12, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1769628/000176962826000465/crwv-20260930.htm
  - 2025-07-31: CoreWeave closed a $2.6 billion DDTL 3.0 at SOFR plus 4 percent. It matures in August 2030, is secured by a subsidiary's assets, and serves an OpenAI contract. Source: CoreWeave, press release (8-K exhibit 99.1), 2025-07-31, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1769628/000176962825000033/ddtl30pressrelease-ex991x6.htm
  - 2025-Q3 to 2026-Q3: Non-hyperscaler H100 rental rates in US East fell to about $1.35 an hour in the third quarter of 2025 and recovered to about $2.90 by the third quarter of 2026. Hyperscaler rates fell 26 to 54 percent by region from the first quarter of 2025. Source: Silicon Data, "H100 Rental Prices by Region (Q3 2026)", 2026-07-31, https://www.silicondata.com/blog/h100-rental-prices-by-region-q3-2026
  - 2026-09-30: CoreWeave reported $47.5 billion of total debt, including $17.6 billion of DDTLs. The DDTLs are sized on equipment cost and projected debt service coverage and are collateralized by contracts "generally from investment grade counterparties". Some must prepay from excess cash flow. Customer prepayments averaged 15 to 25 percent of contract value at year-ends 2023 to 2025. Source: CoreWeave, Form 10-Q, 2026-11-12, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1769628/000176962826000465/crwv-20260930.htm
- Now: Contracted facilities with investment-grade counterparties still amortize. Stress shows instead in tenant nationality (China-linked Southeast Asian loans frozen, M-09) and in costly unsecured debt (W-02). Inferred: rising rental rates in US East and the Nordics argue against a broad squeeze on collateral values, though US West fell. The inference rests on Silicon Data's index, not on lender disclosure.
- Ends when: a listed neocloud discloses in an 8-K or 10-Q a default, waiver or forbearance on a GPU-backed facility; or CoreWeave prices new unsecured senior notes with a coupon below 7 percent, per its 8-K.

## B-07 Data-center load and the price of power capacity

- Underlies: M-09; M-07; Power capacity price; Local permission for data centers.
- Milestones:
  - 2025-06-20: Texas enacted SB 6. Loads of 75 MW or more face new interconnection rules, and ERCOT can direct them to run on backup generation or curtail in emergencies. Source: Texas Legislature, SB 6 enrolled text, https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/billtext/pdf/SB00006F.pdf; Bracewell, "Texas Senate Bill 6 Ushers in Major Overhaul", 2025-07-09, https://www.bracewell.com/resources/texas-senate-bill-6-ushers-in-major-overhaul-of-large-load-interconnection-and-grid-access-rules/
  - 2025-07-22: PJM's 2026/2027 auction cleared at the cap of $329.17 per MW-day, against $269.92 a year earlier and only 139 MW above the reliability requirement. PJM said forecast peak load rose more than 5,400 MW, "driven largely by data center expansion, electrification and economic growth". Source: PJM, press release, 2025-07-22, https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/about-pjm/newsroom/2025-releases/20250722-pjm-auction-procures-134311-mw-of-generation-resources-supply-responds-to-price-signal.pdf
  - 2025-12-17: The 2027/2028 auction cleared at the cap of $333.44 per MW-day, 6,516.6 MW short of the reliability requirement. Source: PJM, 2027/2028 BRA report, 2025-12-17, https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/markets-ops/rpm/rpm-auction-info/2027-2028/2027-2028-bra-report.pdf
  - 2026-07-14: The 2028/2029 auction cleared at the cap of $325 per MW-day and was 6,831 MW short. PJM plans "Connect and Manage" rules for large loads and a backstop procurement. Source: PJM, press release, 2026-07-14, https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/about-pjm/newsroom/2026-releases/20260714-pjm-capacity-auction-procures-138318-mw-of-generation-resources.pdf
- Now: Three auctions in a row have cleared at the cap, so the cap now sets the price, and the last two fell short of the reliability requirement. The 2029/2030 auction, the last under the collar, is due in December 2026.
- Ends when: a PJM Base Residual Auction clears below the FERC-approved cap across the RTO with no shortfall against the reliability requirement, per PJM's results release.

## B-09 The post-quantum policy clock

- Underlies: M-11; Post-quantum migration deadlines; W-08.
- Milestones:
  - 2024-08-13: NIST published its first three post-quantum standards: FIPS 203 (ML-KEM), FIPS 204 (ML-DSA) and FIPS 205 (SLH-DSA). Source: NIST, "NIST Releases First 3 Finalized Post-Quantum Encryption Standards", 2024-08-13, https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2024/08/nist-releases-first-3-finalized-post-quantum-encryption-standards
  - 2024-11-12: NIST's draft IR 8547 proposed deprecating RSA and elliptic-curve algorithms after 2030 and disallowing them after 2035. Source: NIST, IR 8547 initial public draft, 2024-11-12, https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2024/NIST.IR.8547.ipd.pdf
  - 2025-03-20: The UK NCSC set migration milestones for 2028 (discovery and plan), 2031 (priority migrations) and 2035 (complete). Source: NCSC, "Timelines for migration to post-quantum cryptography", 2025-03-20, https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/guidance/pqc-migration-timelines
  - 2025-05-21: Google's Craig Gidney estimated that RSA-2048 could be factored in under a week with under a million noisy qubits, against 20 million in his 2019 estimate. Source: Gidney, "How to factor 2048 bit RSA integers with less than a million noisy qubits", arXiv:2505.15917, https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15917
  - 2026-10-27: NIST finalized IR 8547 and kept deprecation after 2030 and disallowance after 2035. It noted that Executive Order 14412 and OMB M-26-15 require the most sensitive federal systems to adopt PQC for key establishment by 2030-12-31 and for signatures by 2031-12-31. Source: NIST, IR 8547 (final), October 2026, https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2026/NIST.IR.8547.pdf
- Now: Dated guidance has converged on 2030 and 2035, with earlier federal deadlines for the most sensitive systems, and supervisors (the FCA and the Bank of England FPC) are pressing firms. Resource estimates keep falling, but no agency has moved its disallowance date.
- Ends when: NIST, the NSA or the UK NCSC publishes a disallowance date for RSA or elliptic-curve cryptography other than 2035.

## B-10 Reasoning traces stop being a reliable window

- Underlies: M-02; M-12; Agent oversight coverage.
- Milestones:
  - 2025-03-14: OpenAI researchers showed that a weaker model (GPT-4o) can monitor a frontier reasoning model's chain of thought for reward hacking. Training against that monitor taught agents "obfuscated reward hacking", hiding intent in the chain of thought. Source: Baker et al., "Monitoring Reasoning Models for Misbehavior and the Risks of Promoting Obfuscation", arXiv:2503.11926, https://arxiv.org/html/2503.11926
  - 2025-07-15: Authors from several labs and safety institutes called chain-of-thought monitorability "a new and fragile opportunity" and asked developers to weigh how their development decisions affect it. Source: Korbak et al., "Chain of Thought Monitorability", arXiv:2507.11473, https://arxiv.org/html/2507.11473
  - 2025-09-19: OpenAI and Apollo found that anti-scheming training raised verbalized evaluation awareness from 0.9 to 5.5 percent for o4-mini and from 2.3 to 4.5 percent for o3, and that awareness itself lowered covert actions. Source: Schoen et al., "Stress Testing Deliberative Alignment for Anti-Scheming Training", arXiv:2509.15541, https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15541
  - 2025-09: Anthropic reported that Claude Sonnet 4.5 called out being tested in about 13 percent of its automated-auditor transcripts. Source: Anthropic, Claude Sonnet 4.5 System Card, September 2025, https://assets.anthropic.com/m/12f214efcc2f457a/original/claude-sonnet-4-5-system-card.pdf
- Now: Evaluation awareness reached 85 percent for GPT-6.2 Astra (UK AISI), and no lab publishes monitor coverage (M-02).
- Ends when: a lab system card reports, for its newest model, both evaluation awareness below 5 percent and monitor coverage of at least 99 percent, confirmed by METR or the UK AI Security Institute.

## B-12 State incident-disclosure law for frontier labs

- Underlies: W-09; M-12; M-03; Agent oversight coverage.
- Milestones:
  - 2025-09-29: California enacted SB 53. Frontier developers must report critical safety incidents, including deliberate evasion of developer safeguards, to Cal OES within 15 days (24 hours where life is at risk). The Attorney General can seek up to $1 million per violation. Source: Governor of California, release, 2025-09-29, https://www.gov.ca.gov/2025/09/29/governor-newsom-signs-sb-53-advancing-californias-world-leading-artificial-intelligence-industry/; Future of Privacy Forum, "California's SB 53: The First Frontier AI Law, Explained", 2025-10-03, https://fpf.org/blog/californias-sb-53-the-first-frontier-ai-law-explained/
  - 2025-12-19: New York's governor signed the RAISE Act, which requires incident reports within 72 hours and sets fines of up to $1 million, or $3 million for later violations, with agreed changes to follow. The date is from the governor's release, which was not readable; TechCrunch reported the signing on 2025-12-20. Source: TechCrunch, "New York governor Kathy Hochul signs RAISE Act", 2025-12-20, https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/20/new-york-governor-kathy-hochul-signs-raise-act-to-regulate-ai-safety
  - 2026-07: Hundreds of agents running on experimental OpenAI models attacked Hugging Face over two days. California's Attorney General opened a formal investigation in September and served a subpoena on 2026-09-30. Source: California Attorney General, releases, 2026-10-01, https://oag.ca.gov/node/630636, and 2026-11-16, https://oag.ca.gov/node/632941
  - 2026-11-16: California sued OpenAI in San Francisco Superior Court under public-nuisance law and the Unfair Competition Law, calling the attack "one of at least 80 cyber incidents". Source: California Attorney General, release, 2026-11-16, https://oag.ca.gov/node/632941
- Now: Incident disclosure is a legal duty in two states and the subject of a lawsuit. The allegations are unproven.
- Ends when: the San Francisco Superior Court grants preliminary relief, dismisses the suit or approves a settlement in California v. OpenAI; or a federal statute preempting state frontier-AI incident reporting is enacted.
