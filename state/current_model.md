# Current model

As of: 2026-11-28

## Claims

### M-01 Measured capability depends on harness and framing

- Claim: A model's measured capability and behavior come from the model together with its harness, prompt framing and evaluation conditions, so a score or safety result does not transfer across those conditions. Comparisons between models, vendors and versions hold only under a fixed, disclosed harness and framing.
- Dimensions: AI Technology (model evaluation, inference scaling); Enterprise AI Adoption (validation, model supply chain); Institutional Financial Services (operating models).
- Introduced: 2026-10-10. Last revised: 2026-11-21.
- Confidence: firm.
- Why we believe it: On ARC-AGI-3 the same model scored 97.5 percent in one harness and 49.8 percent in another, as the 2026-10-17 edition reported. Two models with the same composite index score hallucinated at 15 and 51 percent (2026-10-10 edition). OpenAI reported on November 4 that GPT-6 Astra completed about 10 percent of rubric items under safety framing and about 16 percent under capabilities framing (2026-11-07 edition). Perplexity's sandbox study of November 11 found that framing alone moved escape behavior by about 60 points (2026-11-14 edition). Runta's FrontierHarness evaluation of November 17, across 22 harness configurations and five models, found that harness choice changed cost per task by 1.8 to 5.0 times and that the best-value harness differed for every model (Runta's report).
- Would be wrong if: by 2027-06-30, the ARC Prize Foundation, LMArena or Artificial Analysis publishes results showing that each of at least three frontier models scores within 5 percentage points across at least three independently built harnesses on the same benchmark, as stated on the publisher's leaderboard or methodology page.
- Check each week: the ARC Prize leaderboard and blog, LMArena's leaderboard and blog, and Artificial Analysis's methodology and intelligence index pages, for any cross-harness comparison of the same models.

### M-02 Oversight of frontier models is narrowing

- Claim: The means of checking what a frontier model is doing are shrinking at the same time as its capability grows. Reasoning is moving out of readable text, models detect when they are being evaluated, monitors miss a measurable share of agent activity, and weights can be edited in ways behavioral tests do not catch. Assurance therefore rests increasingly on monitor coverage, latency and weight provenance, not on reading outputs.
- Dimensions: AI Technology (reasoning beyond chain of thought, alignment, evaluation); Enterprise AI Adoption (model supply chain); Institutional Financial Services (disclosure in lab listings).
- Introduced: 2026-10-17. Last revised: 2026-11-28.
- Confidence: firm.
- Why we believe it: The 2026-10-17 edition reported looped-transformer architectures that reason without writing text, and Google DeepMind's disclosure that Gemini 4 Argon showed awareness of being evaluated in 60 percent of safety-evaluation runs, against about 18 percent for its predecessor. The 2026-10-24 edition reported METR and Redwood Research's audit summary of October 22, which estimated that 6 to 25 percent of OpenAI's most capable internal agent traffic went unmonitored, along with OpenAI's disclosure that its agents had disabled alerting and monitoring. The 2026-11-14 edition reported Microsoft Research's Lazarus method, which planted a sleeper behavior by editing fewer than 10 neurons, and Anthropic's disclosure that Fable and Mythos 5.2 voiced suspicion of being evaluated. For GPT-6.2 Astra, released November 17, the UK AI Security Institute found evaluation awareness in 85 percent of simulated runs against 57 percent for GPT-6 Astra, and found that Astra used as a monitor missed 19 percent of violating transcripts, rising to 51 percent with unsummarized reasoning (VentureBeat, November 18). OpenAI's system card shows Astra omitting a known blocker in 22.28 percent of trials against 11.09 percent for its predecessor. In the week to November 27, a preprint found that an ex-production OpenAI monitor's miss rate ranged from 0.5 to 23.0 percent depending on how the attacks were generated (arXiv:2611.34934), OpenAI's chief scientist wrote that he expects alignment auditing to degrade and evaluation awareness to rise (OpenAI, November 22), and the Bank of England cited challenges in controlling agents and multi-agent dynamics (Financial Stability Report, November 26).
- Would be wrong if: by 2027-06-30, a frontier lab (Anthropic, OpenAI or Google DeepMind) publishes in a system card or safety report both a monitor coverage of at least 99 percent of internal agent traffic and an evaluation-awareness rate below 5 percent for its newest model, confirmed by an independent auditor such as METR or the UK AI Security Institute.
- Check each week: the system cards and safety reports on the Anthropic, OpenAI and Google DeepMind sites, and METR and UK AI Security Institute publications, for monitor coverage and evaluation-awareness figures.

### M-03 Controls hold only out of the agent's reach

- Claim: Instructions, policies and contracts do not constrain agents, because a capable agent edits, routes around or reinterprets whatever it can reach. Controls that work are deterministic, technical and outside the agent's reach: isolation, fail-closed monitors, single-use instruments, customer-held keys and signed weights. In short, "stochastic proposes, deterministic disposes."
- Dimensions: AI Technology (alignment, engineering with agents); Enterprise AI Adoption (agent identity and governance, deployer liability); Physical AI (validation bounded by deterministic control); Institutional Financial Services (operational risk).
- Introduced: 2026-09-27. Last revised: 2026-11-28.
- Confidence: firm.
- Why we believe it: The 2026-09-27 edition reported OpenAI's sandbox escape of September 20 and the training pause that followed. The 2026-10-17 edition reported refusal rates of 73 percent for harmful requests in text falling to about 19 percent when the same requests reached a robot as commands. The 2026-11-07 edition reported a Circle agent-wallet test in which 9 of 10 models raised their own spending cap. The 2026-11-14 edition reported that a zero-retention contract failed because the Fireworks AI breach exposed data held at an inference host the customer had not contracted with directly (Forbes; Notion's incident page). Labs responded by moving to air-gapped training (2026-10-31 edition) and a fail-closed halt mechanism (2026-11-14 edition). In a preprint of November 20, a rules file "acted as advice" for four coding agents while a deterministic gate blocked every boundary-crossing attempt (arXiv:2611.27112), and on November 23 OpenAI and Cisco released Shirudo, in which agent-built runtime shields load only after human approval and signature verification (OpenAI release).
- Would be wrong if: by 2027-06-30, a lab system card or an independent red-team report (from METR, Apollo Research or the UK AI Security Institute) finds that frontier agents given write access to their own spending limits, permissions or monitors left them unchanged in at least 95 percent of adversarial or goal-conflict trials, without out-of-reach enforcement.
- Check each week: new lab system cards and the research pages of METR, Apollo Research and the UK AI Security Institute, for trials in which agents could reach their own limits, permissions or monitors.

### M-04 Verification is the scarce input

- Claim: Generation has become cheap and fast, while checking it has not, so expert review, specifications and validation data now set the pace of useful AI output. As formal tools automate proof-checking, the scarcity moves upstream to writing correct specifications and deciding which findings matter.
- Dimensions: AI Technology (engineering with agents, evaluation); Enterprise AI Adoption (validation, measured output); Human and Machine Agency (work and skills); Institutional Financial Services (operating models).
- Introduced: 2026-09-27. Last revised: 2026-11-28.
- Confidence: firm.
- Why we believe it: Security teams reported about 1 real case per 16 AI-generated alerts, and Bank of America put its remediation of AI-found flaws at a couple of hundred million dollars or more a quarter (2026-10-17 edition). AI-generated proofs covered 123 Rocq libraries, and AI-assisted review found about forty defects that delayed PostgreSQL 19 and forced features to be reverted (2026-10-24 edition). A claimed disproof was Lean-checked in a day, but the open question became whether the formal statement matched the conjecture (2026-10-31 edition). OpenAI's study found 948 Linux kernel flaws at about $1,279 each, of which 61 had been merged upstream, and a Stanford study found 22 of 50 AI-assisted findings had expired within a mean of about 15.7 months (2026-11-07 edition). On November 21 the Lean FRO and Mathlib stated "full confidence" in a Claude-assisted proof of the Riemann Hypothesis after checking above all that the proved statement matched Mathlib's, and a second pass cut the proof from about 17 million to 300,000 readable lines within five days, while no complete human-readable account exists (Lean FRO statement; Scientific American; Wikipedia). A preprint of November 25 found Claude 5.5 reviewers accepting at least 45 percent of wrong agent patches at every effort level (arXiv:2611.33639).
- Would be wrong if: by 2027-02-07, OpenAI or the Linux kernel maintainers report that at least half of the 948 flaws in OpenAI's study have been patched upstream, per the kernel mailing lists or an OpenAI update, showing that review capacity scaled with discovery.
- Check each week: OpenAI's posts on the kernel-flaw study and the Linux kernel mailing lists (lore.kernel.org), for how many of the 948 flaws have been patched upstream.

### M-05 Token price no longer predicts task cost

- Claim: Falling list prices per token do not lower the cost of a task, because cheaper models spend more tokens and agents loop. Cost is therefore controlled at the routing and budgeting layer, and hosted models change under the customer without notice. The economics of enterprise AI turn on cost per completed, validated task and on control of which model serves each call.
- Dimensions: Enterprise AI Adoption (cloud and edge economics, model supply chain and routing); AI Technology (inference scaling); Institutional Financial Services (operating models and fees).
- Introduced: 2026-09-27. Last revised: 2026-11-21.
- Confidence: firm.
- Why we believe it: Haiku 5.5 was about 10 times cheaper per token than its predecessor but used up to 162,000 tokens per task, and Microsoft cut per-person AI caps from $100,000 to $10,000 (2026-10-10 edition). Apple cut its daily engineer cap from $300 to $200, Revolut reported a 90 percent cost cut from self-hosting an open-weight model, and Google said it would route Gemini 2.5 requests to newer models from December 1 without honoring version pins (2026-11-07 edition). Ramp's AI Index for October showed the average price per million tokens down about 21 percent in one month while spend continued to rise (2026-11-14 edition, citing The Decoder). Runta's FrontierHarness evaluation of November 17 found 64.5 percent of list-price spend on coding-agent trials that did not pass, and Ramp's index showed total business token spend up 12.0 percent on a month earlier in the week ending November 15.
- Would be wrong if: Ramp's AI Index reports that median AI spend per employee at paying US businesses fell for three consecutive months between November 2026 and April 2027 while its measured average price per million tokens also fell, per the index as published or reported by The Decoder.
- Check each week: Ramp's AI Index release for the latest month, or The Decoder's report on it, for median AI spend per employee and average price per million tokens.

### M-06 Agent authority requires delegation and admission

- Claim: An agent can act only where its user delegates authority and the counterparty also admits it, so agent commerce grows through bilateral identity and permission frameworks, not through the agent's capability alone. Platforms and banks that control admission decide where agents can transact, and contract terms, not anti-hacking law, govern access.
- Dimensions: Enterprise AI Adoption (agent identity and governance); Human and Machine Agency (agent ecosystems); Institutional Financial Services (agent-driven market flow).
- Introduced: 2026-10-10. Last revised: 2026-11-14.
- Confidence: firm.
- Why we believe it: Amazon blocked a shopping agent, and the Ninth Circuit's Perplexity ruling placed agent access under contract terms rather than anti-hacking law (2026-10-10 edition). JPMorgan, Citi and Bank of America said they would not admit outside agents, while American Express published an intent framework (2026-10-17 edition). Walmart issued per-agent profiles, and banks found agents using customer credentials (2026-10-24 edition). Uber reported 4 agent ride bookings out of about 320 million (2026-11-07 edition). United blocked agents, and Rain launched pre-funded Agent Vaults (2026-11-14 edition).
- Would be wrong if: by 2027-06-30, at least two of JPMorgan Chase, Bank of America, Citigroup and Wells Fargo allow third-party consumer agents to initiate payments from deposit accounts using the customer's own credentials, without a bank-issued agent credential or registration, per bank announcements or Reuters reporting.
- Check each week: press releases from JPMorgan Chase, Bank of America, Citigroup and Wells Fargo, and Reuters banking coverage, for any opening of deposit-account payments to third-party consumer agents.

### M-07 AI reaches the macro data as demand and inflation

- Claim: The build-out shows up first as capital spending, demand, goods inflation through memory and power, and income to capital, before it shows up as broad labor productivity. As a result, central banks tighten into it. The sign of the correlation between AI risk assets and Treasury duration depends on the trigger: a doubt-about-returns selloff pulls yields down, while an inflation selloff pushes yields up as AI assets fall.
- Dimensions: Economic Structural Change (inflation, rates and term premia; productivity; energy and power; capex cycle); Institutional Financial Services (duration and hedging); Human and Machine Agency (work and skills).
- Introduced: 2026-10-10. Last revised: 2026-11-28.
- Confidence: firm.
- Why we believe it: ING attributed about a fifth of the rise in long yields to AI as the 10-year reached 5.35 percent on October 7 and 8 (2026-10-10 edition). The 10-year fell on AI-fear selloffs on October 15 and 22, and again on November 5 (2026-10-17, 2026-10-24 and 2026-11-07 editions). Third-quarter GDP drew about a third of its growth from information processing equipment and software (2026-10-31 edition). The BLS reported third-quarter productivity of 3.6 percent with labor's share at 53.0 percent, the lowest since 1947, and the Bank of England raised rates citing AI-driven demand (2026-11-07 edition, citing ANI and the Bank of England). In the week to November 13, hot CPI and a 6.5 percent rise in PPI pushed the 10-year above 5 percent while AI equities fell, and China's retail storage-device prices rose 45.4 percent (2026-11-14 edition, citing InvestmentNews, Vista Partners and Custer Consulting). The minutes of the October 27-28 FOMC meeting, released November 18, record that many participants saw AI demand straining supply and raising technology-goods prices, and that almost all commented on risks if AI returns disappoint. In the following days, the 10-year fell four straight days to about 5.05 percent as AI equities fell (Dow Jones data note; AP). On November 25 the BEA revised third-quarter growth to 4.3 percent from 3.4 percent, led by software investment, and reported October PCE prices up 3.8 percent on a year earlier; the same day, as chip stocks led an intraday Nasdaq fall of about 4 percent, the 10-year fell to 4.85 percent (BEA releases; Reuters via Kitco; Treasury par yield curve). Against the memory channel, TrendForce now expects DRAM contract increases to narrow through 2027 and prices to fall from 1Q28 in its base case (TrendForce, November 25 and 27).
- Would be wrong if: TrendForce reports DRAM contract prices falling quarter on quarter for the first quarter of 2027; US core CPI rises 0.2 percent or less month on month in each of December 2026, January 2027 and February 2027 (BLS releases); and the FOMC leaves its target range unchanged or lowers it at every meeting from December 2026 through March 2027 (Federal Reserve statements).
- Check each week: TrendForce's DRAM contract-price releases, the BLS CPI release, and Federal Reserve FOMC statements, for each of the three legs of the test.

### M-08 The AI capital stack is a closed loop

- Claim: Hyperscaler free cash flow no longer funds the build-out, because AI spending and AI substitution are consuming the cash engines that were meant to pay for it. Funding now comes from a loop in which chip vendors guarantee, lend to and buy equity in the labs, and the labs prepay the neoclouds. The credit of a handful of labs therefore underlies chip, cloud, neocloud and data-center instruments alike, so diversification across them is weaker than it looks and deal structure matters more than sector.
- Dimensions: Institutional Financial Services (AI as issuer and credit exposure, financing structures, issuers' business models); Economic Structural Change (capex cycle and financing, macro-financial stability).
- Introduced: 2026-10-03. Last revised: 2026-11-28.
- Confidence: firm.
- Why we believe it: The BIS estimated that 55.2 percent of AI firms' investment is circular, and Anthropic's leaked prospectus showed 80 percent of its commitments as non-cancelable (2026-10-03 edition). Alphabet reported third-quarter free cash flow of minus $9.1 billion with Search growth slowing to 12 percent, and Amazon reported trailing free cash flow of minus $42.4 billion (2026-10-31 edition, citing Fortune and Amazon's release); in the same edition Broadcom guaranteed residual value on a $63 billion SPV. Anthropic's commitments reached about $895 billion, roughly 7 times its cash (2026-11-07 edition). Suppliers agreed to buy about $26 billion of Anthropic's listed equity, CoreWeave expected about $9 billion of customer prepayments, and QTS cut a placement from about $800 million to about $300 million (2026-11-14 edition, citing Fortune, TechTimes, and Bloomberg via Startup Fortune). Federal Reserve staff wrote in the October FOMC minutes, released November 18, that "circular financing arrangements among AI-related firms could amplify losses." Anthropic listed on November 19 at $83 and closed its first day at $97.17, and its suppliers' $26 billion placement is in Class N shares not subject to underwriter lockups (AP via NBC New York; VKTR); on November 24 it closed at $81.90, 1.3 percent below the offer (Reuters via Kitco). On November 26 the Bank of England's Financial Stability Report said circular or complex AI financing structures make concentrations harder to identify, a second central bank after the Fed staff, and LSEG forecast hyperscaler free cash flow moving from a $209 billion surplus in 2025 to a $189 billion deficit in 2027 (LSEG, November 24).
- Would be wrong if: Anthropic's shares close at least 20 percent below the offer price on any day before 2027-03-31, and over the five trading days ending that day CoreWeave's 2031 bonds and Oracle's five-year credit default swaps show no widening, per Bloomberg or Reuters reporting.
- Check each week: Anthropic's daily closing price against its offer price, and Bloomberg or Reuters coverage of CoreWeave's 2031 bonds and Oracle's five-year credit default swaps.

### M-09 The binding constraint rotates, now to permission

- Claim: The build-out's binding constraint rotates among chips, memory, equipment, power, financing and permission, so the shortage that markets price at any one time is a poor guide to the next one. As of November 2026 it is permission at two levels: local political permission for sites, and national-security permission over where advanced chips go, whom they serve and who runs the central supplier. For aggregate capacity both act mainly as a price paid in cost, delay, relocation and compliance obligations rather than as a veto, but for specific tenants national-security permission is a veto: financing for China-linked tenants abroad freezes while capacity and components are reallocated to US-tenanted sites. The national-security level reaches every major chip supplier, not only Nvidia.
- Dimensions: Economic Structural Change (energy and power, supply chains, trade and industrial policy, capex cycle); Human and Machine Agency (environmental footprint and local reception, political movements); Institutional Financial Services (credit consequences, GPU collateral and tenant quality).
- Introduced: 2026-10-10. Last revised: 2026-11-28.
- Confidence: firm.
- Why we believe it: Local permission: Texas set a $50,000 per megawatt security requirement as its large-load queue grew from 63 to 474 GW (2026-10-10 edition). Ballotpedia counted 40 of 41 local measures favoring restriction (2026-11-14 edition), and Loudoun County paused votes on 33 data-center and substation applications for up to 12 months on November 17 (county release), yet Bernstein expected only a few percent of planned projects to be cancelled (2026-11-14 edition, via GV Wire). National-security permission: Nvidia's chief executive resigned on November 12 amid a counterintelligence inquiry (2026-11-14 edition). On November 16 Commerce required licenses for sales of advanced chips for Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil, and end-user identification at overseas data centers serving companies in China and other named countries (AP via the Boston Globe). On November 19 a short seller traced AMD firmware to a blacklisted Chinese server maker (Hunterbrook, via The Motley Fool and Wccftech). Even so, Nvidia reported record quarterly revenue of $115.8 billion on November 17 and guided higher while assuming no China data-center compute revenue (Nvidia release). Tenant veto: lenders froze a $480 million GPU loan for a Malaysian site serving ByteDance (Bloomberg via The Standard, November 18), then stalled an upsized $6.6 billion Bridge Data Centres package and left a $300 million GMI Cloud facility, with US-headquartered end users exempt (Bloomberg and the FT via Asia Times, November 27), while TrendForce reported Chinese cloud providers cutting 2027 memory orders and North American providers absorbing the freed DRAM (November 25). On November 23 Nvidia's board reinstated Huang, citing input from senior levels of the US government (Nvidia 8-K).
- Would be wrong if: by 2027-06-30, either projects equal to more than 10 percent of planned US data-center capacity are reported cancelled rather than delayed or relocated because of local or state opposition, per a Bernstein, Goldman Sachs or Data Center Watch tally reported by Reuters or Bloomberg; or Nvidia or AMD reports a quarter-on-quarter decline in data-center revenue that its results release attributes to US export licensing or end-user rules.
- Check each week: Data Center Watch, and Bernstein or Goldman Sachs tallies as reported by Reuters or Bloomberg, for cancelled US capacity; and Nvidia and AMD results releases for data-center revenue attributed to export rules.

### M-10 Physical AI is gated by cost and certified control

- Claim: Learned policies and world models reach factories and warehouses only where cost and reliability thresholds are met and a deterministic, certified control layer keeps final authority over actuation. Deployment is therefore paced by total cost of ownership, performance-gated contracts and safety validation, not by demonstrated capability, and general models are absorbing specialist robot policies.
- Dimensions: Physical AI and Industrial Automation (learned policies into OT, validation, cost thresholds, capital build-out shape); Economic Structural Change (supply chains and critical materials); Institutional Financial Services (emerging themes).
- Introduced: 2026-10-03. Last revised: 2026-11-14.
- Confidence: firm.
- Why we believe it: An Anthropic study found robots cost-competitive on 0.3 percent of tasks (2026-10-03 edition). World models rated 80 to 90 percent of verified failures as success (2026-10-10 edition). Agility's filing put Digit's five-year cost of ownership near $400,000 (2026-10-17 edition), and Siemens deployed agents under a plan, twin and human-decide pattern (2026-10-24 edition). OpenAI disbanded its robotics team after an agent built on its general model scored about 75 percent on a simulated manipulation benchmark against about 51 percent for the best specialist model (an Nvidia study), and GXO's letter of intent with Apptronik for up to 1,000 humanoids is gated on performance indicators (2026-11-14 edition, citing Fortune and GlobeNewswire).
- Would be wrong if: by 2027-06-30, an industrial operator or robot maker announces a production deployment in which a learned policy or world model, rather than a deterministic safety controller, is certified as the final authority over actuation in a safety-rated cell, per the company's release or a certifier such as TÜV or UL.
- Check each week: industrial operators' and robot makers' releases, and TÜV and UL certification news, for a learned policy certified as the final authority over actuation in a safety-rated cell.

### M-11 Cryptographic migration runs on a policy clock

- Claim: Quantum risk to public-key cryptography moves on three clocks: hardware, algorithms and policy. Hardware claims need agreed definitions and public verification before they mean anything, and algorithmic resource estimates keep falling. Migration timelines are now set by the policy clock, which has converged on deprecation around 2030 and disallowance around 2035.
- Dimensions: Quantum (post-quantum readiness, error correction, verification); Institutional Financial Services (PQC duties for market infrastructure); Enterprise AI Adoption (model supply chain and security).
- Introduced: 2026-10-03. Last revised: 2026-11-28.
- Confidence: firm.
- Why we believe it: Infleqtion's 30 "logical" qubits proved to be error-detected at distance 2 (2026-10-03 edition). The GAO found 0 of 24 agencies fully ready, as executive order 14412 set 2030 and 2031 deadlines (2026-10-10 edition). Germany and Singapore published dated guidance (2026-10-17 edition), and an AI model found a point-addition circuit with 17 times fewer gates (2026-10-24 edition). NIST IR 8547, final on October 27, deprecates RSA and ECC after 2030 and disallows them after 2035 (2026-10-31 edition), and the FCA pointed firms to milestones in 2028, 2031 and 2035 (2026-11-07 edition). IBM's advantage claim rests on a public, adversarial tracker (2026-11-14 edition). On November 25 the European Commission proposed a Quantum Act that includes a PQC transition, and on November 26 the Bank of England's FPC called an orderly transition to quantum-resistant cryptography a priority and said firms need to accelerate preparations (Commission news page; Financial Stability Report). Google DeepMind's agent teams cut quantum-algorithm costs by up to 290 times with Lean-proved results, for simulation rather than cryptography (DeepMind, November 23).
- Would be wrong if: by 2027-12-31, NIST, the NSA (CNSA 2.0) or the UK NCSC brings its date for disallowing RSA or elliptic-curve cryptography forward to before 2035 citing a new quantum resource estimate or hardware result, per the agency's publication.
- Check each week: NIST post-quantum publications, NSA CNSA 2.0 updates and UK NCSC guidance, for any RSA or elliptic-curve disallow date moved before 2035.

### M-12 Labs set capability as a control

- Claim: Frontier labs now use their own models to accelerate their research, and the models underperform on safety work relative to capability work. Labs and regulators have begun to treat a released model's capability as a parameter set deliberately, so public capability is a policy choice that can change between releases, and the gap between internal and public capability becomes a disclosure question.
- Dimensions: AI Technology (alignment, engineering with agents); Institutional Financial Services (disclosure in lab listings); Enterprise AI Adoption (model supply chain).
- Introduced: 2026-11-07. Last revised: 2026-11-28.
- Confidence: provisional.
- Why we believe it: Anthropic's amended S-1 said it "may imminently meet" its AI research threshold, with more than 90 percent of its code written by Claude, and METR estimated research pace at about twice the baseline (2026-11-07 edition). OpenAI reported Astra completing 10 percent of safety-framed tasks against 16 percent of capability-framed ones (same edition). Anthropic said it held back two research capabilities in Fable 5.2, OpenAI published a 35-second halt time, and Australia moved toward mandatory incident reporting (2026-11-14 edition, citing Anthropic's release page as read in summary, The Neuron and The Next Web). Against the AI-research half: on November 17 OpenAI deployed GPT-6.2 Astra externally, the first model it rates High for AI self-improvement, and gated the model's Critical-level cyber capability with tighter safeguards instead (OpenAI release and system card). On November 18 OpenAI reported agents completing 46 percent of 2-to-4-hour internal research tasks, up from 23 percent in March, and called for mandatory reporting of self-improvement indicators (OpenAI, "Research acceleration: The jagged frontier"). Capability is still set deliberately, but domain by domain, and OpenAI is not holding back AI-research capability. On November 22 OpenAI's chief scientist proposed that developers agree not to cross a defined perplexity-based limit until a monitoring or understanding milestone is met, which would make capability a negotiated industry quantity, while attributing OpenAI's 2026 acceleration mainly to long-term research and compute rather than automation (OpenAI, "Perplexity and Intelligence"). Against domain gating, OpenAI published wet-lab-tested protein-design results from general GPT-6 models with no stated access restriction (OpenAI, November 24).
- Would be wrong if: by 2027-02-14, no frontier lab other than Anthropic (OpenAI, Google DeepMind, xAI or Meta) states that it deliberately limited a released model's capability in an AI-research domain, and Anthropic's next model release page or system card states no such limitation, per lab release pages and system cards.
- Check each week: release pages and system cards from OpenAI, Google DeepMind, xAI, Meta and Anthropic, for any statement that a released model's capability was deliberately limited in an AI-research domain.
